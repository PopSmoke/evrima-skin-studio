COLOR AND INTERACTION DISCOVERY

These methods come from this project's decoded-material experiments, not an
official modding specification. Bright-looking surface color is not proof of
emission, lighting nearby objects, or visibility at night in the live game.

WHAT THE LOCAL STUDIES SUPPORT

1. Raise one strong channel while keeping secondary channels bounded.
   In the Bright-Color-Study, raising every RGB channel by 1e8 washed many
   previews toward white. Raising only the leading channel retained much more
   vivid coverage. Modest gains can retain more detail than enormous values.
   Try changing the leading channel through 1, 8, 64, 4096, 1e8 and 1e22 while
   the remaining channels stay around .004, .08, .35 or 1. These are search
   inputs, not guaranteed display colors. Bright blue can have low luminance;
   use Vivid color ranking as well as Bright area.

2. Use a negative source section to reshape a positive target section.
   Body/flank, body/underbelly and markings/flank are useful starting pairs in
   the existing study. Body slots feed a shared shader with overlapping masks.
   A very negative contribution can cancel positive color in overlap regions;
   clipping sharpens the resulting boundaries. This can expose broken or
   speckled detail without selecting a separate pixelation shader.
   A previously reproduced cyan example on Carno and Omni, adult male,
   patterns 1 and 2, standard model, variation 8:
     b = [-999999, -999999, -999999]
     f = [0.004, 8, 8]
     md, m, u, d = [0.01, 0.01, 0.01]
     alpha = 1; original eyes enabled.
   This is a decoded-preview reproduction, not confirmed live-game parity.

3. Signed channels are worth testing, but are not a general brightness boost.
   Push the weakest channel below zero while stronger channels stay positive.
   Compare with a positive-only control. A previous moderate-negative sweep
   showed little average brightness benefit, so judge results rather than
   assuming that a more negative number is better.

4. Probe representable neighbors around extreme values.
   Float32 spacing increases with magnitude: around 999999 it is .0625; around
   9999999 it is 1. Small contributions can be lost in subtraction/addition.
   Neighbor probes can reveal sudden changes or demonstrate that several
   different numbers produce identical pictures. This is rounding behavior,
   not a reliable new-color multiplier. Sex and growth blends also matter.

USING EXPERIMENT LAB > DISCOVER

- Choose your dinosaur, pattern, sex, growth and lighting before opening the lab.
  The lab freezes that context. Unrelated sections retain your current colors.
- Isolated bright channels and Signed channel contrasts change only the target
  section, producing 1-24 candidates. Pick a family or try All families.
- Negative mask pairs holds the source at the entered negative level and tries
  positive target colors. Try b -> f, b -> u, or m -> f. Change the source level
  between batches (e.g. -1, -100, -4096, -999999, -9999999 or -1e8).
- Precision neighbors uses the closest float32 value and neighboring values
  around the entered center, with one fixed target color. All families uses
  cyan for this method so color changes do not confound the neighbor comparison.
- Pair methods allow 1-8 candidates. Each has Source only, Target only, and
  Combined controls; all share a Baseline. At most 25 images are held in memory.
- Existing channel locks and alpha are preserved. Eyes are never changed by
  discovery. A seed repeats numeric candidates; it does not guarantee the same
  picture on a different model, pattern, camera, renderer or lighting setup.
- Run experiments, then Rank completed results. Baseline remains first;
  candidates precede their controls. Original order restores generation order.
- Vivid color rewards saturated bright pixels and penalizes white clipping.
  Bright area measures linear luminance. Least white is useful for avoiding
  washout but can rank a dark skin highly; inspect the preview too.
- Interaction strength measures the display-image residual:
     (combined - source-only) - (target-only - baseline).
  It reports estimated visible model pixels above 8/255 in any channel. This
  includes downstream lighting, clipping and sampling; it is not an exclusive
  measure of mask overlap or proof of a new shader. Missing controls produce no
  interaction score. Other scores also use an approximate background mask.
- Identical pictures are flagged. Different raw numbers are not necessarily
  different visible colors. The displayed average vivid HEX is a measured
  screenshot color, not an equivalent skin import value.
- Favorite interesting results, export named skin JSONs or keep them in My skin
  library. Save findings JSON preserves exact documents, context, captured
  locks, scores and thresholds without storing image assets.
- Save a recipe to repeat the method on another pattern or dinosaur. Old
  pre-discovery recipes remain readable. Recipes contain settings, not results.
- Apply a promising result, inspect it at higher preview detail, try other
  patterns/sex/growth, then record an actual in-game comparison in My skin
  library. Low-resolution noise alone is weak evidence of a repeatable effect.

EVIDENCE IN THE DEVELOPMENT PROJECT
Bright-Color-Study/README.txt: 192 recipes / 576 renders plus 108 import retests.
Negative-Body-Flank/Explanation.txt: shader masks and controlled reproductions.
Negative-9999999-Study/Explanation.txt: 704 contexts / 142,912 evaluations and
float32 diagnostics. These studies are bounded tests, not exhaustive coverage.
The portable release includes this guide; the larger study folders remain in
the development project to avoid increasing download size.
